🧠 Important Concepts

1. Industry

Industry refers to human activity involved in converting raw materials into readily usable products.
2. Industrialisation

Industrialisation refers to the process of using modern production techniques to produce goods on a large scale for consumers and other producers.
3. Importance of Industrialisation

Industrialisation contributes to economic development, employment, production, income generation and expansion of related activities.
4. Industrial Cluster

Industrial clusters are groups of firms located in a defined geographic area that share common markets, technologies and skill requirements.
5. Geographical Proximity

Successful clusters generally have small and medium enterprises located close to one another.
6. Consumer Goods Industry

Industries producing goods that are consumed by final consumers are called consumer goods industries.
7. Capital Goods Industry

Industries producing goods used by other producers in production are called capital goods industries.
8. Basic Goods Industry

Industries producing raw materials or basic inputs required by other industries are called basic goods industries.
9. Chennai Automobile Cluster

Chennai is a major automobile manufacturing centre and is popularly known as the "Detroit of Asia".
10. Coimbatore Cluster

Coimbatore is well known for engineering industries, including pumpsets, motors and machinery.
11. Tiruppur Cluster

Tiruppur is widely known for its knitwear industry and garment-related production.
12. Leather Cluster

Areas including Ambur and Vaniyambadi are important centres associated with leather processing and leather goods.
13. Sivakasi

Sivakasi is an important industrial centre particularly associated with printing and fireworks-related industries.
14. SIPCOT

SIPCOT is the State Industries Promotion Corporation of Tamil Nadu, an important organisation involved in industrial development.
15. TIDCO

TIDCO is the Tamil Nadu Industrial Development Corporation and works to promote industrial development and investment.
16. Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur is an innovator of new ideas and business processes who organises resources and takes business decisions.
17. Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship can contribute to capital formation, employment, innovation, production and economic development.
18. Industrial Development

Industrial development can create employment opportunities and support related businesses and services.

🏭 Industrial Cluster Flow

A cluster brings related firms, workers, suppliers, skills and supporting institutions closer together.

🏢 Firms
↓ Production and business activities
👷 Skilled Workers
↓ Shared labour and skills
⚙️ Suppliers
↓ Raw materials, components and supporting services
🛒 Markets
↓ Buyers and business connections
📈 Economic Development
Employment + production + entrepreneurship

🧮 Important Calculations

Percentage = Part ÷ Whole × 100
Percentage Increase = Increase ÷ Original Value × 100
Percentage Decrease = Decrease ÷ Original Value × 100
Total Production = Production of all units added together
Average Production = Total Production ÷ Number of Units
Employment Growth = New Employment − Old Employment
Employment Growth % = Employment Increase ÷ Old Employment × 100

🔢 Numerical Application Sums

Example 1: An industrial cluster has 200 firms. If 25% of them are new firms, how many new firms are there?

25 ÷ 100 × 200
= 50 firms
Example 2: A cluster has 400 firms. If 15% are engineering firms, how many engineering firms are there?

15 ÷ 100 × 400
= 60 firms
Example 3: A cluster had 5,000 workers. Employment increased to 6,000. Find the increase.

6,000 − 5,000
= 1,000 workers
Example 4: Employment increased from 5,000 to 6,000 workers. Find the percentage increase.

Increase = 1,000
1,000 ÷ 5,000 × 100
= 20%
Example 5: Four firms produce 1,000, 1,500, 2,000 and 2,500 units.

Total production:
1,000 + 1,500 + 2,000 + 2,500
= 7,000 units
Example 6: A cluster has 10 firms. Each firm employs 50 workers.

Total employment:
10 × 50
= 500 workers
Example 7: An industrial area has 800 workers. 30% work in manufacturing.

30 ÷ 100 × 800
= 240 workers
Example 8: A cluster has 120 firms. 40 firms are small enterprises. What percentage are small enterprises?

40 ÷ 120 × 100
= 33.33% approximately
Example 9: Production increased from 20,000 units to 25,000 units.

Increase = 5,000
Percentage increase:
5,000 ÷ 20,000 × 100
= 25%
Example 10: A cluster contains 500 firms. 60% are small and medium enterprises.

60 ÷ 100 × 500
= 300 firms
