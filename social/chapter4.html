🧠 Important Concepts

1. Post-War World

World War II brought major political and economic changes. European colonial powers weakened and many Asian and African countries moved towards independence.
2. Chinese Revolution

The Chinese Communist Revolution resulted in the establishment of the People's Republic of China in 1949 under Mao Zedong.
3. Mao Zedong

Mao Zedong was the leader of the Chinese Communist movement and became the leading figure of the People's Republic of China.
4. People's Republic of China

The People's Republic of China was established in 1949 after the Communist victory in China.
5. Cold War

The Cold War was a prolonged political, ideological and military rivalry mainly between the United States and the Soviet Union.
6. Truman Doctrine

The Truman Doctrine represented the United States policy of supporting countries resisting the spread of communism.
7. Marshall Plan

The Marshall Plan provided economic assistance for the reconstruction of European countries after World War II.
8. NATO

The North Atlantic Treaty Organization was formed as a military alliance among Western countries.
9. Warsaw Pact

The Warsaw Pact was a military alliance formed by the Soviet Union and its Eastern European allies in response to Western military alliances.
10. Korean War

Korea was divided into North and South. The Korean War began in 1950 and continued for three years.
11. Third World Countries

Countries outside the two major Cold War power blocs were generally described as Third World countries.
12. Non-Aligned Movement

The Non-Aligned Movement emerged from the newly independent countries' desire to avoid military alignment with either Cold War superpower.
13. Bandung Conference

The Bandung Conference was held in Indonesia in 1955 and brought together newly independent Asian and African countries.
14. Belgrade Conference

The first formal conference of the Non-Aligned Movement was held at Belgrade in 1961.
15. Cuban Revolution

The Cuban Revolution brought Fidel Castro to power in Cuba and changed Cuba's relationship with the United States and Soviet Union.
16. Cuban Missile Crisis

The Cuban Missile Crisis occurred in 1962 when the United States discovered Soviet nuclear missiles being installed in Cuba.

🗓️ Important Events

1945: World War II ended and a new international political order began to emerge.
1947: The Truman Doctrine was announced and the United States adopted a policy of containment towards communism.
1948: The Marshall Plan was implemented to assist European reconstruction.
1949: The People's Republic of China was established.
1949: NATO was established.
1950: The Korean War began.
1953: The Korean War ended with an armistice.
1955: The Bandung Conference was held in Indonesia.
1961: The first Non-Aligned Movement conference was held at Belgrade.
1961: The Bay of Pigs invasion took place in Cuba.
1962: The Cuban Missile Crisis brought the United States and Soviet Union close to nuclear conflict.
