🧠 Important Concepts

1. Human Geography

Human geography studies the development of human societies and their relationship with the physical environment.
2. Agriculture

Agriculture includes cultivation of crops, rearing of animals and birds, forestry, fisheries and related activities.
3. Agricultural Landforms

The plains of Tamil Nadu are generally more suitable for agriculture because of favourable relief and fertile soils. The Cauvery plains are an important agricultural region.
4. Rice

Rice is the major food crop of Tamil Nadu. Important rice-growing areas include the Cauvery delta and other suitable plains.
5. Millets

Millets form an important food crop in Tamil Nadu and are cultivated in several relatively dry regions.
6. Plantation Crops

Tea, coffee, rubber, cashew and cinchona are important plantation crops associated with Tamil Nadu.
7. Tea

Tea plantations are mainly found in the hill regions, particularly the Nilgiris and surrounding highlands.
8. Coffee

Coffee is grown mainly in the hill regions of the Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats.
9. Water Resources

Tamil Nadu depends on rivers, reservoirs, tanks, groundwater and rainfall for its water requirements.
10. Dams

Dams are used for irrigation, water storage and, in suitable locations, hydro-electric power generation.
11. Mettur Dam

Mettur is an important dam associated with the Cauvery River and plays an important role in irrigation and water management.
12. Sathanur Dam

Sathanur Dam is constructed across the Thenpennai River and is an important irrigation project.
13. Mineral Resources

Tamil Nadu has mineral resources such as limestone, magnesite, granite, lignite and other minerals that support various industries.
14. Lignite

Neyveli is an important lignite-producing region of Tamil Nadu.
15. Industries

Important industries of Tamil Nadu include textiles, automobiles, leather, cement, sugar, engineering, chemicals and information technology.
16. Textile Industry

Tamil Nadu is an important textile and garment-producing state. Coimbatore and Tiruppur are major centres.
17. Automobile Industry

Chennai and its surrounding industrial region have developed into an important automobile manufacturing centre.
18. Population

Population refers to the number of people living in a particular area. Its distribution depends on factors such as relief, water, agriculture, industries and employment opportunities.
19. Urbanisation

Industrialisation and employment opportunities encourage migration and urban growth.
20. Transport

Roadways, railways, waterways, airways and seaways connect different parts of Tamil Nadu and support economic activity.
21. Ports

Tamil Nadu has major and minor ports along its eastern coastline. Ports support imports, exports and international trade.
22. Communication

Communication systems such as telecommunications, internet services and mass media support economic and social development.
23. Trade

Trade involves the exchange of goods and services. Tamil Nadu participates in both internal and international trade.
24. Fisheries

Marine fishing is important along the coastal areas while inland fishing is practised in suitable rivers, reservoirs, tanks and ponds.
25. Man-made Disasters

Industrial accidents, fires, chemical accidents, pollution and transport accidents are examples of disasters caused or intensified by human activities.

🔢 Numerical Practice

Example 1: A farmer owns 20 hectares of land. If 60% is used for rice cultivation, how much land is used for rice?

20 × 60 ÷ 100 = 12 hectares
Example 2: A reservoir contains 80,000 million litres of water. If 25% is used for irrigation, how much water is used?

80,000 × 25 ÷ 100 = 20,000 million litres
Example 3: A district has a population of 10 lakh people. If 40% live in urban areas, how many people live in urban areas?

10,00,000 × 40 ÷ 100 = 4,00,000 people
Example 4: An industrial area produces 50,000 tonnes of goods. If 30% are exported, how many tonnes are exported?

50,000 × 30 ÷ 100 = 15,000 tonnes
Example 5: A transport company travels 600 km in 10 hours. What is its average speed?

Speed = Distance ÷ Time
600 ÷ 10 = 60 km/h
Example 6: A farmer produces 4,000 kg of rice. If 15% is used for seeds, how much rice is used for seeds?

4,000 × 15 ÷ 100 = 600 kg
