🧠 Important Concepts

1. Constitution

A Constitution is the fundamental law of a country. It defines the structure and powers of government and protects the rights of citizens.
2. Need for a Constitution

A Constitution provides a framework for government, limits governmental power, protects citizens' rights and establishes the basic principles of governance.
3. Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly was responsible for framing the Constitution of India.
4. Drafting Committee

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar served as the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.
5. Adoption of the Constitution

The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949.
6. Constitution Came into Force

The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.
7. Preamble

The Preamble expresses the ideals and objectives of the Constitution. It describes India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic.
8. Justice

The Preamble promises social, economic and political justice.
9. Liberty

The Preamble assures liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship.
10. Equality

The Constitution seeks equality of status and opportunity.
11. Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights protect important freedoms and rights of citizens.
12. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Article 32 provides the Right to Constitutional Remedies. It enables citizens to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
13. Directive Principles

Directive Principles of State Policy provide guidelines to the State for establishing social and economic justice.
14. Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties remind citizens of their responsibilities towards the nation and society.
15. Citizenship

Citizenship establishes the legal relationship between an individual and the State.
16. Citizenship Act, 1955

The Citizenship Act, 1955 deals with matters relating to Indian citizenship.
17. Acquisition of Citizenship

Indian citizenship may be acquired through methods provided by law, including birth, descent, registration, naturalisation and incorporation of territory.
18. Loss of Citizenship

Citizenship can be lost according to provisions of law, including renunciation, termination and deprivation.
19. Federal Features

India has a federal system with constitutional division of powers between the Union and the States.
20. Centre-State Relations

Centre-State relations include legislative, administrative and financial relations.
21. Legislative Relations

Legislative powers are distributed between the Union and States through constitutional provisions.
22. Administrative Relations

Administrative relations determine how the Union and States coordinate in administration.
23. Financial Relations

Financial relations concern the distribution of revenues and financial responsibilities between the Union and States.
24. Official Language

The Constitution contains provisions concerning the official language of the Union and the use of languages in administration.
25. Constitutional Amendment

The Constitution provides a procedure for making amendments so that it can adapt to changing circumstances.
26. Emergency Provisions

The Constitution contains provisions for dealing with extraordinary situations through different types of emergencies.
27. Constitutional Committees

Various committees and commissions have contributed to constitutional governance and institutional development.
