🧠 Important Concepts

1. Location of Tamil Nadu

Tamil Nadu is located in the southern part of India. Its latitudinal extent is about 8°4'N to 13°35'N and its longitudinal extent is about 76°18'E to 80°20'E.
2. Area

Tamil Nadu covers about 1,30,058 square kilometres and is one of the large states of India by area.
3. Western Ghats

The Western Ghats form an important mountain system along the western side of Tamil Nadu. The Nilgiris, Anaimalai and Palani hills are important hill groups associated with this region.
4. Eastern Ghats

The Eastern Ghats in Tamil Nadu are discontinuous and are dissected by east-flowing rivers. Javadhu, Kalrayan and Servarayan are important hill groups.
5. Doddabetta

Doddabetta is the highest peak in the Nilgiri Hills and is an important physical feature of Tamil Nadu.
6. Solaikaradu

Solaikaradu in the Servarayan Hills is identified in the textbook material as the highest peak of the Eastern Ghats in Tamil Nadu.
7. Plains

The plains of Tamil Nadu can broadly be considered as inland plains and coastal plains. Rivers have played an important role in their formation.
8. Cauvery River

Cauvery is one of the major rivers of Tamil Nadu. Its delta is an important agricultural region.
9. Thamirabarani River

Thamirabarani is an important perennial river of southern Tamil Nadu. Its tributaries include Karaiyar, Servalar, Manimuthar, Gadananathi, Pachaiyar, Chittar and Ramanathi.
10. Soil

Soil is loose material formed mainly through weathering and erosion of rocks. It provides minerals and nutrients needed for vegetation and agriculture.
11. Red Soil

Red soil contains iron oxides and is widely distributed in several parts of Tamil Nadu.
12. Laterite Soil

Laterite soil occurs in areas such as parts of the Nilgiris and northern districts. It can support crops such as tea and coffee in suitable regions.
13. Natural Vegetation

Natural vegetation is influenced by landforms, soil, temperature, rainfall and relief.
14. Mangrove Forests

Mangroves occur in coastal areas and river deltas. Important locations in Tamil Nadu include Pichavaram, Muthupet and Vedaranyam.
15. Climate

Tamil Nadu receives an important share of its rainfall from the Northeast Monsoon.
16. Tropical Cyclones

Tropical cyclones commonly affect Tamil Nadu during the Northeast Monsoon season and may bring heavy rainfall to coastal areas.
17. Water Conservation

Rainwater harvesting, groundwater management, renovation of traditional water sources and recycling of water are important conservation measures.
18. Kallanai

Kallanai is an ancient water-regulation structure associated with the Cauvery River and is traditionally attributed to Karikalan.
19. Biosphere Reserves

Important biosphere reserves associated with Tamil Nadu include the Nilgiris Biosphere Reserve, Gulf of Mannar Biosphere Reserve and Agasthiyarmalai Biosphere Reserve.
20. Disaster Risk Reduction

Disaster Risk Reduction involves reducing exposure to hazards, reducing vulnerability, managing land and environment wisely and improving preparedness and early warning.

🔢 Numerical Practice

Example 1: Tamil Nadu has an area of approximately 1,30,058 km². If a map represents 10% of this area, what area does it represent?

1,30,058 × 10 ÷ 100 = 13,005.8 km²
Example 2: A rainfall station records 800 mm of rainfall in one season. Another station records 600 mm. What is the difference?

800 − 600 = 200 mm
Example 3: A reservoir contains 50,000 million litres of water. If 20% is used, how much water is used?

50,000 × 20 ÷ 100 = 10,000 million litres
Example 4: A forest area is 40,000 hectares. If 5% is affected by a natural hazard, what area is affected?

40,000 × 5 ÷ 100 = 2,000 hectares
Example 5: A river basin receives 1,200 mm of rainfall and another receives 900 mm. What is the percentage difference relative to 900 mm?

Difference = 1,200 − 900 = 300 mm

300 ÷ 900 × 100 = 33.33%
