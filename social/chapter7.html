🧠 Important Concepts

1. Revolt of 1857

The Revolt of 1857 was a major uprising against British East India Company rule. It began at Meerut and spread to several parts of northern and central India.
2. Immediate Cause

The introduction of the new Enfield rifle cartridges became the immediate cause of the revolt because soldiers believed that the cartridges were greased with cow and pig fat.
3. Mangal Pandey

Mangal Pandey was an Indian soldier associated with the early events that preceded the Revolt of 1857.
4. Bahadur Shah II

Bahadur Shah II was accepted as the symbolic emperor by the rebels during the Revolt of 1857.
5. Rani Lakshmibai

Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi became one of the prominent leaders of the Revolt of 1857.
6. Nana Sahib

Nana Sahib was one of the important leaders associated with the Revolt of 1857 at Kanpur.
7. Tantia Tope

Tantia Tope was an important military leader who continued resistance against British forces during the revolt.
8. Begum Hazrat Mahal

Begum Hazrat Mahal played a leading role in the resistance at Lucknow during the Revolt of 1857.
9. Peasant Movements

Peasant resistance developed against oppressive land revenue systems, landlords, moneylenders and colonial economic policies.
10. Farazi Movement

The Farazi Movement was launched by Haji Shariatullah in East Bengal in 1818 and later developed under Dudu Mian.
11. Dudu Mian

Dudu Mian mobilised peasants and opposed exploitation by landlords and colonial authorities.
12. Wahabi Movement

The Wahabi movement became associated with resistance to colonial authority and efforts to reform Muslim society.
13. Tribal Uprisings

Several tribal communities resisted British policies, moneylenders, landlords and the disruption of traditional systems.
14. Growth of Nationalism

Political associations, newspapers, education, social reform and economic criticism helped develop a broader sense of Indian nationalism.
15. Economic Critique

Indian nationalists criticised colonial economic policies for causing poverty, exploitation and the drain of wealth from India.
16. Drain of Wealth

Nationalist thinkers argued that wealth generated in India was being transferred to Britain through various colonial mechanisms.
17. Indian National Congress

The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885 and became an important organisation in the growth of Indian nationalism.
18. Allan Octavian Hume

Allan Octavian Hume played an important role in the formation of the Indian National Congress.
19. W.C. Bonnerjee

W.C. Bonnerjee presided over the first session of the Indian National Congress held in Bombay in 1885.
20. Early Nationalists

Early nationalists used constitutional methods such as petitions, speeches, meetings and representations to express Indian demands.
21. Extremist Nationalism

A more assertive form of nationalism emerged in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, demanding stronger political action.

🗓️ Important Dates and Events

1818: Haji Shariatullah launched the Farazi Movement in East Bengal.
1857: The major Revolt of 1857 broke out against British East India Company rule.
10 May 1857: The revolt began at Meerut.
1858: The Government of India Act transferred administration from the East India Company to the British Crown.
1870–1885: Period associated with the growth of political associations and the foundation of the Indian National Congress.
1885: The Indian National Congress was founded.
1885: The first session of the Indian National Congress was held at Bombay.
72: The first session of the Indian National Congress had 72 delegates.
