🧠 Important Concepts

1. Gross Domestic Product

GDP is the market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country during a specified period.
2. National Income

National income represents the income earned by the factors of production of a country during a particular period.
3. GDP at Market Price

GDP at market price measures the value of final goods and services at market prices.
4. Primary Sector

The primary sector is directly dependent on natural resources. Agriculture, fishing, forestry and mining are examples.
5. Secondary Sector

The secondary sector converts raw materials into finished or semi-finished products. Manufacturing and construction are examples.
6. Tertiary Sector

The tertiary sector provides services. Banking, transport, education, healthcare and communication are examples.
7. GDP Growth

GDP growth refers to the increase in the value of goods and services produced by an economy over time.
8. Economic Development

Economic development involves improvement in income, employment, living standards and other socio-economic conditions.
9. Employment

Employment provides people with opportunities to participate in productive economic activities and earn income.
10. Final Goods

Final goods are goods purchased for final use and are not intended for further processing or resale as intermediate goods.
11. Intermediate Goods

Intermediate goods are used as inputs in the production of other goods and services.
12. Value Added

Value added is the additional value created during the production process.
13. Nominal GDP

Nominal GDP measures production using current market prices.
14. Real GDP

Real GDP measures production using constant prices, helping to identify changes in actual output.
15. Per Capita Income

Per capita income is calculated by dividing national income by the population.
16. Economic Policies

Economic policies are measures adopted by the government to influence economic activity, growth and development.

🧮 Important Formulas

GDP Growth Rate = [(Current GDP − Previous GDP) ÷ Previous GDP] × 100
Per Capita Income = National Income ÷ Population
Value Added = Value of Output − Intermediate Consumption
Percentage = (Part ÷ Total) × 100

🔢 Numerical Sums

Example 1: Previous GDP = ₹10 lakh crore
Current GDP = ₹11 lakh crore

Growth rate = (11 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100
= 1 ÷ 10 × 100
= 10%
Example 2: Previous GDP = ₹20 lakh crore
Current GDP = ₹22 lakh crore

Growth rate = (22 − 20) ÷ 20 × 100
= 10%
Example 3: National Income = ₹50,000 crore
Population = 10 crore

Per capita income = 50,000 ÷ 10
= ₹5,000
Example 4: Value of output = ₹10,000 crore
Intermediate consumption = ₹4,000 crore

Value added = 10,000 − 4,000
= ₹6,000 crore
Example 5: Current GDP = ₹15 lakh crore
Previous GDP = ₹12 lakh crore

Growth rate = (15 − 12) ÷ 12 × 100
= 3 ÷ 12 × 100
= 25%
Example 6: Primary sector contribution = ₹200 crore
Secondary sector contribution = ₹300 crore
Tertiary sector contribution = ₹500 crore

Total contribution = 200 + 300 + 500
= ₹1,000 crore
Example 7: Total GDP = ₹1,000 crore
Tertiary sector = ₹500 crore

Percentage contribution = 500 ÷ 1000 × 100
= 50%
Example 8: Total GDP = ₹2,000 crore
Primary sector = ₹400 crore

Primary sector contribution = 400 ÷ 2000 × 100
= 20%
Example 9: GDP increases from ₹50 crore to ₹60 crore.

Increase = 60 − 50 = ₹10 crore
Growth rate = 10 ÷ 50 × 100
= 20%
Example 10: Population = 5 crore
National Income = ₹25,000 crore

Per capita income = 25,000 ÷ 5
= ₹5,000

📈 GDP Growth

GDP growth measures how the production of an economy changes over time. Higher production can contribute to economic growth, although GDP alone does not capture every aspect of people's well-being.
