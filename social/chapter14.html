🧠 Important Concepts

1. Natural Resources

Natural resources are materials and substances obtained from nature that are useful to human beings.
2. Renewable Resources

Renewable resources can be naturally replenished over time. Examples include sunlight, wind and water.
3. Non-Renewable Resources

Non-renewable resources are available in limited quantities and take a very long time to form. Examples include coal, petroleum and minerals.
4. Mineral Resources

Minerals are naturally occurring substances found in the Earth's crust and are important raw materials for many industries.
5. Metallic Minerals

Metallic minerals contain metals and can be processed to obtain useful metallic materials. Examples include iron ore, manganese and bauxite.
6. Non-Metallic Minerals

Non-metallic minerals do not yield metals as their main product. Limestone and mica are examples.
7. Iron Ore

Iron ore is an important mineral resource used as a basic raw material for the iron and steel industry.
8. Manganese

Manganese is an important mineral used in metallurgical industries, especially in steel production.
9. Bauxite

Bauxite is the principal ore used for obtaining aluminium.
10. Mica

Mica is a non-metallic mineral with excellent insulating properties and is useful in electrical and electronic applications.
11. Limestone

Limestone is an important raw material used in cement manufacturing.
12. Coal

Coal is an important fossil fuel and is widely used for power generation and industrial purposes.
13. Petroleum

Petroleum is a major fossil fuel used for transportation, industry and the production of many petroleum products.
14. Natural Gas

Natural gas is a relatively clean fossil fuel used for electricity generation, industrial purposes and domestic needs.
15. Thermal Power

Thermal power is generated by using heat energy, commonly from fuels such as coal.
16. Hydroelectric Power

Hydroelectricity is generated using the energy of flowing or falling water.
17. Nuclear Energy

Nuclear energy is released through nuclear reactions and can be used to generate electricity.
18. Industry

Industry refers to economic activities involved in processing raw materials and producing goods.
19. Agro-Based Industries

Agro-based industries use agricultural products as their main raw materials. Cotton textile, sugar and food-processing industries are examples.
20. Mineral-Based Industries

Mineral-based industries use minerals as their major raw materials. Iron and steel is an important example.
21. Cotton Textile Industry

The cotton textile industry uses cotton fibre as its major raw material and produces yarn, cloth and other textile products.
22. Iron and Steel Industry

Iron and steel is a basic industry because its products are used as raw materials by many other industries.
23. Industrial Location

Industries are located based on factors such as raw materials, power, labour, transport, markets and capital.
24. Industrial Development

Industrial development contributes to employment, production, infrastructure, trade and economic growth.

🔢 Numerical Practice

Example 1: A factory produces 8,000 tonnes of steel in a year and increases production by 1,500 tonnes.

New production:

8,000 + 1,500 = 9,500 tonnes
Example 2: A factory produces 12,000 units using 4 production lines.

Production per line:

12,000 ÷ 4 = 3,000 units
Example 3: A power plant generates 6,000 MW of electricity from 3 plants.

Average generation:

6,000 ÷ 3 = 2,000 MW
Example 4: A mineral reserve contains 50,000 tonnes. If 20,000 tonnes are extracted, how much remains?

50,000 − 20,000 = 30,000 tonnes
Example 5: Industrial production increases from 20,000 tonnes to 25,000 tonnes.

Increase:

25,000 − 20,000 = 5,000 tonnes

Percentage increase:

(5,000 ÷ 20,000) × 100 = 25%
