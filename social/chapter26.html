🧠 Important Concepts

1. Role of Government

The government performs important economic functions such as providing public goods, maintaining infrastructure, collecting revenue and supporting social welfare.
2. Public Goods

Public goods are goods or services that are generally provided for the benefit of society, such as public infrastructure and certain public services.
3. Public Revenue

Public revenue refers to the income received by the government from taxes and other sources.
4. Tax Revenue

Tax revenue is the income collected by the government through different forms of taxation.
5. Non-Tax Revenue

Non-tax revenue includes government income from sources other than taxes, such as fees, fines and certain service-related receipts.
6. Direct Tax

A direct tax is imposed directly on an individual or organisation and is generally paid directly to the government.
7. Indirect Tax

An indirect tax is imposed on goods or services and is generally collected through transactions.
8. Progressive Tax

Under a progressive tax system, the tax burden increases as the taxable income increases.
9. Proportional Tax

A proportional tax applies the same tax rate to the taxable amount.
10. Regressive Tax

A regressive tax can place a relatively greater burden on lower-income groups.
11. GST

GST means Goods and Services Tax. It is a tax applied to the supply of goods and services under the GST system.
12. Public Expenditure

Public expenditure is government spending on public services, infrastructure, welfare and other government activities.
13. Government Budget

A government budget presents planned government receipts and expenditure for a financial period.
14. Fiscal Policy

Fiscal policy involves government decisions concerning taxation, public expenditure and related economic activities.
15. Importance of Taxes

Taxes provide revenue that enables the government to finance public services, infrastructure and welfare activities.
16. Taxpayer

A taxpayer is a person or entity that is liable to pay taxes according to applicable tax rules.

Government revenue can be used to finance public expenditure and provide services to society.

🧮 Important Calculations

Tax Amount = Taxable Amount × Tax Rate ÷ 100
Total Amount = Original Amount + Tax
Amount Before Tax = Total Amount − Tax
Tax Rate = Tax Amount ÷ Taxable Amount × 100
Government Revenue = Tax Revenue + Non-Tax Revenue
Budget Balance = Government Revenue − Government Expenditure
Percentage Change = Change ÷ Original Value × 100

🔢 Numerical Sums

Example 1: Taxable amount = ₹10,000
Tax rate = 10%

Tax = 10,000 × 10 ÷ 100
= ₹1,000
Example 2: Original price = ₹5,000
Tax rate = 18%

Tax = 5,000 × 18 ÷ 100
= ₹900

Total = 5,000 + 900
= ₹5,900
Example 3: Taxable amount = ₹20,000
Tax = ₹2,000

Tax rate = 2,000 ÷ 20,000 × 100
= 10%
Example 4: Tax revenue = ₹50 crore
Non-tax revenue = ₹20 crore

Total government revenue = 50 + 20
= ₹70 crore
Example 5: Government revenue = ₹100 crore
Government expenditure = ₹80 crore

Budget balance = 100 − 80
= ₹20 crore surplus
Example 6: Government revenue = ₹75 crore
Government expenditure = ₹90 crore

Balance = 75 − 90
= −₹15 crore
This represents a ₹15 crore deficit.
Example 7: Price = ₹8,000
Tax rate = 5%

Tax = 8,000 × 5 ÷ 100
= ₹400

Total = 8,000 + 400
= ₹8,400
Example 8: Taxable income = ₹50,000

= ₹6,000
