🧠 Important Concepts

1. Mahatma Gandhi

Gandhi emerged as one of the most influential leaders of the Indian national movement and promoted non-violent methods of resistance.
2. Satyagraha

Satyagraha means holding firmly to truth. Gandhi used it as a method of non-violent resistance against injustice.
3. Champaran Satyagraha

Gandhi's Champaran Satyagraha was associated with the problems faced by indigo cultivators in Bihar.
4. Ahmedabad Mill Strike

Gandhi supported workers during the Ahmedabad mill strike and used Satyagraha as a method of resolving the dispute.
5. Kheda Satyagraha

The Kheda Satyagraha was connected with the demand for suspension of land revenue because of crop failure.
6. Rowlatt Act

The Rowlatt Act gave the colonial government extensive powers to suppress political activities and detain people.
7. Jallianwala Bagh

On 13 April 1919, General Dyer ordered troops to fire on a gathering at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar.
8. Khilafat Movement

The Khilafat Movement developed around the issue of the Ottoman Caliphate and became associated with the wider nationalist movement.
9. Non-Cooperation Movement

Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement to encourage Indians to withdraw cooperation from colonial institutions.
10. Chauri Chaura

The Chauri Chaura incident in 1922 led Gandhi to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement because of the use of violence.
11. Simon Commission

The Simon Commission was opposed in India because it did not include an Indian member.
12. Purna Swaraj

Purna Swaraj means complete independence. The Indian National Congress adopted complete independence as its goal at the Lahore session.
13. Salt Satyagraha

Gandhi began the Salt March from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi in 1930 to challenge the colonial salt laws.
14. Civil Disobedience Movement

The Civil Disobedience Movement involved deliberately breaking unjust colonial laws through non-violent methods.
15. Gandhi-Irwin Pact

The Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed in 1931 between Mahatma Gandhi and Lord Irwin.
16. Round Table Conferences

The Round Table Conferences were held in London to discuss constitutional reforms concerning India.
17. Quit India Movement

The Quit India Movement was launched in August 1942 with the demand for an end to British rule in India.
18. Cripps Mission

The Cripps Mission came to India during World War II to discuss constitutional proposals with Indian leaders.
19. Cabinet Mission

The Cabinet Mission came to India in 1946 to discuss the transfer of power and a constitutional framework.
20. Towards Independence

The nationalist movement, mass participation and political negotiations eventually led India towards independence in 1947.

🗓️ Important Dates and Events

1915: Mahatma Gandhi returned to India from South Africa.
1917: Champaran Satyagraha took place in Bihar.
1918: Ahmedabad Mill Strike and Kheda Satyagraha were important Gandhian movements.
1919: Rowlatt Act and the Jallianwala Bagh massacre became major turning points.
13 April 1919: Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place at Amritsar.
1920: Non-Cooperation Movement was launched.
1922: Chauri Chaura incident occurred and Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement.
1929: Lahore Congress adopted Purna Swaraj as the goal of the national movement.
1930: Gandhi launched the Salt Satyagraha.
1931: Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed.
1942: Quit India Movement was launched.
1946: Cabinet Mission came to India.
1947: India became independent.
