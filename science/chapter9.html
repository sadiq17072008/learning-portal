🧠 Important Concepts

1. Solution

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances.
2. Solute

The substance that is dissolved in a solution is called the solute.

Example: In salt solution, salt is the solute.
3. Solvent

The substance in which the solute dissolves is called the solvent.

Example: In salt solution, water is the solvent.
4. Aqueous Solution

A solution in which water acts as the solvent is called an aqueous solution.
5. Dilute Solution

A solution containing a relatively small amount of solute is called a dilute solution.
6. Concentrated Solution

A solution containing a relatively large amount of solute is called a concentrated solution.
7. Saturated Solution

A solution which contains the maximum amount of solute that can dissolve at a particular temperature is called a saturated solution.
8. Unsaturated Solution

A solution which can dissolve more solute at a particular temperature is called an unsaturated solution.
9. Supersaturated Solution

A solution containing more dissolved solute than the normal saturation concentration at a given temperature is called a supersaturated solution.
10. Solubility

Solubility is the maximum amount of solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a specified temperature.
11. Factors Affecting Solubility

Important factors affecting solubility include:

• Nature of solute and solvent
• Temperature
• Pressure, especially for gases
12. Concentration

Concentration indicates the amount of solute present in a given amount of solution or solvent.

🧮 Numerical Examples

Example 1 – Mass Percentage

A solution contains 10 g of solute and 90 g of solvent. Find the mass percentage of solute.
Mass of solution = 10 + 90 = 100 g
Mass % = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 100
Mass % = (10 / 100) × 100
Answer: 10%
Example 2 – Finding Solute Mass

Find the mass of solute required to prepare 200 g of a 10% solution.
Mass of solute = (10 / 100) × 200
Answer: 20 g
Example 3 – Finding Solution Mass

A solution contains 25 g of solute and has a concentration of 5% by mass. Find the mass of the solution.
Mass % = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 100
5 = (25 / Mass of solution) × 100
Answer: 500 g
Example 4 – Percentage Concentration

A solution contains 15 g of salt in 150 g of solution. Calculate the mass percentage.
Mass % = (15 / 150) × 100
Answer: 10%
Example 5 – Solute and Solvent

A solution contains 20 g of sugar and 180 g of water. Find the mass percentage of sugar.
Mass of solution = 20 + 180 = 200 g
Mass % = (20 / 200) × 100
Answer: 10%
