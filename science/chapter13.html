🧠 Important Concepts

1. Plant Anatomy

Plant anatomy is the study of the internal structure of plants and their organs such as roots, stems and leaves.
2. Plant Tissues

A tissue is a group of cells that are similar in structure and perform a particular function.

Plant tissues are broadly classified into:
• Meristematic tissue
• Permanent tissue
3. Meristematic Tissue

Meristematic tissues consist of actively dividing cells. They are responsible for the growth of plants.

Important types include:
• Apical meristem
• Lateral meristem
• Intercalary meristem
4. Apical Meristem

Apical meristem is present at the tips of roots and shoots. It is responsible mainly for increase in the length of the plant.
5. Lateral Meristem

Lateral meristem is responsible mainly for the increase in the girth or thickness of stems and roots.
6. Permanent Tissue

Permanent tissues are formed from meristematic cells that have lost the ability to divide and become specialized to perform specific functions.
7. Parenchyma

Parenchyma is a simple permanent tissue. Its cells are generally living and perform functions such as storage and photosynthesis.
8. Collenchyma

Collenchyma provides mechanical support to growing parts of plants while allowing flexibility.
9. Sclerenchyma

Sclerenchyma provides strength and mechanical support. Its cells generally have thick, lignified walls.
10. Xylem

Xylem is a complex permanent tissue that mainly transports water and minerals from the roots towards other parts of the plant.
11. Phloem

Phloem is a complex permanent tissue that transports prepared food from leaves to other parts of the plant.
12. Stomata

Stomata are tiny pores generally present in the epidermis of leaves. They help in gaseous exchange and transpiration.
13. Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants prepare food using carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
14. Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water in the form of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants, mainly through stomata.
15. Respiration

Respiration is the process in which food molecules are broken down to release energy required for various life processes.

🧮 Important Facts

Example 1 – Photosynthesis

Green plants prepare glucose using carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.
Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen
Example 2 – Xylem

Xylem transports water and dissolved mineral nutrients mainly from the roots towards the aerial parts of the plant.
Example 3 – Phloem

Phloem transports the organic food materials produced by photosynthesis to different parts of the plant.
Example 4 – Transpiration

Water vapour is lost from the aerial parts of plants, especially through stomata.
Example 5 – Stomata

Stomata help plants exchange gases with the atmosphere and also play an important role in transpiration.
