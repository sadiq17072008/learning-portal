🔬 Important Concepts

1. Environment
The environment includes living organisms and the physical surroundings in which they live.
2. Ecosystem
An ecosystem is a functional unit in which living organisms interact with one another and with their physical environment.
3. Biotic Components
Biotic components are the living components of an ecosystem, such as plants, animals and microorganisms.
4. Abiotic Components
Abiotic components are non-living factors such as air, water, soil, temperature, light and minerals.
5. Food Chain
A food chain represents the transfer of food and energy from one organism to another.
6. Food Web
A food web consists of interconnected food chains within an ecosystem.
7. Energy Flow
Energy enters an ecosystem mainly through producers and passes through different trophic levels.
8. Producers
Producers such as green plants prepare food through photosynthesis and form the first trophic level.
9. Consumers
Consumers obtain energy by feeding on plants or other organisms.
10. Decomposers
Decomposers break down dead organic matter and help recycle nutrients in ecosystems.
11. Biodiversity
Biodiversity refers to the variety of life forms present in a particular region or on Earth.
12. Genetic Diversity
Genetic diversity refers to variations in genetic characteristics among individuals of the same species.
13. Species Diversity
Species diversity refers to the variety of different species present in an area.
14. Ecosystem Diversity
Ecosystem diversity refers to the variety of ecosystems and habitats in a region.
15. Biodiversity Conservation
Biodiversity conservation involves protecting species, habitats and ecosystems for present and future generations.
16. In-situ Conservation
In-situ conservation protects organisms within their natural habitats.
17. Ex-situ Conservation
Ex-situ conservation protects organisms outside their natural habitats, such as in zoos, botanical gardens and seed banks.
18. Pollution
Pollution is the undesirable change in physical, chemical or biological characteristics of the environment.
19. Air Pollution
Air pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate the atmosphere.
20. Water Pollution
Water pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate water bodies and reduce water quality.
21. Soil Pollution
Soil pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate soil and affect its quality.
22. Solid Waste Management
Solid waste management involves collection, segregation, treatment, recycling and safe disposal of solid waste.
23. Reduce, Reuse and Recycle
The 3R approach helps reduce waste and conserve natural resources.
24. Sustainable Development
Sustainable development aims to meet present needs while protecting resources and the environment for future generations.

🧠 Important Facts

Ecosystem: An ecosystem contains both biotic and abiotic components.
Producers: Green plants are major producers because they prepare food through photosynthesis.
Food Chain: A food chain shows the transfer of food and energy between organisms.
Decomposers: Decomposers help return nutrients to the ecosystem by breaking down dead matter.
Biodiversity: Biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.
In-situ Conservation: Species are protected in their natural habitats.
Ex-situ Conservation: Species are conserved outside their natural habitats.
Pollution: Pollution can affect air, water, soil, organisms and ecosystems.
Waste Management: Waste segregation, recycling and proper disposal help reduce environmental pollution.
Sustainable Development: Sustainable practices help conserve natural resources for future generations.
