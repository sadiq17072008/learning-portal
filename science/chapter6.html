🧠 Important Concepts

1. Atomic Nucleus

The nucleus is the small, dense central part of an atom. It contains protons and neutrons.
2. Proton

A proton is a positively charged particle present inside the nucleus.
3. Neutron

A neutron is an electrically neutral particle present inside the nucleus.
4. Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers.
5. Radioactivity

Radioactivity is the spontaneous disintegration of an unstable atomic nucleus with the emission of radiation.
6. Alpha Radiation

Alpha radiation consists of helium nuclei. It has relatively low penetrating power.
7. Beta Radiation

Beta radiation consists of high-speed electrons or positrons emitted during radioactive decay.
8. Gamma Radiation

Gamma radiation is electromagnetic radiation with very high penetrating power.
9. Nuclear Fission

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into smaller nuclei with the release of energy.
Heavy nucleus → Smaller nuclei + Energy
10. Nuclear Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the combination of light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus with the release of a large amount of energy.
Light nuclei → Heavy nucleus + Energy
11. Half-Life

Half-life is the time required for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay.
12. Nuclear Energy

Nuclear energy is the energy released during nuclear reactions.

🧮 Numerical Examples

Example 1 – Half-Life

A radioactive substance has a half-life of 10 years. If its initial amount is 100 g, the amount after one half-life is:
Remaining amount = 100 ÷ 2
Answer: 50 g
Example 2 – Second Half-Life

A sample initially contains 80 g of radioactive material. Its half-life is 5 years. Find the amount remaining after 10 years.
After 5 years = 80 ÷ 2 = 40 g
After 10 years = 40 ÷ 2 = 20 g
Answer: 20 g
Example 3 – Half-Life

If the half-life of a substance is 2 hours, how many half-lives occur in 6 hours?
Number of half-lives = 6 ÷ 2
Answer: 3 half-lives
