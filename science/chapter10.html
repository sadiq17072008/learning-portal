🧠 Important Concepts

1. Chemical Reaction

A chemical reaction is a process in which one or more substances are converted into new substances with different properties.
2. Combination Reaction

A reaction in which two or more reactants combine to form a single product is called a combination reaction.
A + B → AB
Example:
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
3. Decomposition Reaction

A reaction in which a single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances is called a decomposition reaction.
AB → A + B
4. Thermolysis

Thermal decomposition is called thermolysis. The decomposition takes place due to heat.
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
5. Photolysis

Decomposition caused by light is called photolysis.
2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂
6. Electrolysis

Decomposition caused by passing electricity through a substance is called electrolysis.
7. Single Displacement Reaction

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.
A + BC → AC + B
Example:
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
8. Double Displacement Reaction

Two compounds exchange their ions to form two new compounds.
AB + CD → AD + CB
9. Precipitation Reaction

A double displacement reaction in which an insoluble solid is formed is called a precipitation reaction.
AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
10. Neutralisation Reaction

A reaction between an acid and a base that produces salt and water is called a neutralisation reaction.
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
11. Reversible Reaction

A reaction that can proceed in both forward and backward directions is called a reversible reaction.
A + B ⇌ C + D
12. Irreversible Reaction

A reaction that proceeds mainly in one direction and cannot be easily reversed is called an irreversible reaction.
13. Chemical Equilibrium

In a reversible reaction, equilibrium is reached when the forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates.
14. Rate of Reaction

The rate of a chemical reaction indicates how quickly reactants are converted into products.
15. Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

Important factors include:

• Concentration
• Temperature
• Surface area
• Catalyst
• Nature of reactants
16. Catalyst

A catalyst changes the rate of a chemical reaction without being permanently consumed in the reaction.
17. pH

pH is a measure related to the hydrogen ion concentration of an aqueous solution.
pH = -log[H⁺]
18. pH Scale

The pH scale commonly ranges from 0 to 14.

pH < 7 → Acidic
pH = 7 → Neutral
pH > 7 → Basic
19. Ionic Product of Water

For water at ordinary conditions:
Kᵥ = [H⁺][OH⁻]
20. Important pH Applications

pH is important in soil, digestion, blood, tooth decay, agriculture and environmental studies.

🧮 Numerical Examples

Example 1 – pH of a Strong Acid

Find the pH of a 0.01 M HCl solution.
pH = -log[H⁺]
pH = -log(10⁻²)
Answer: pH = 2
Example 2 – pOH

The hydroxyl ion concentration of a solution is 1 × 10⁻⁹ M. Find its pOH.
pOH = -log[OH⁻]
pOH = -log(10⁻⁹)
Answer: pOH = 9
Example 3 – Relationship Between pH and pOH

If the pOH of a solution is 11.76, find its pH.
pH + pOH = 14
pH = 14 - 11.76
Answer: pH = 2.24
Example 4 – Ionic Product

At 25°C, if:
[H⁺] = 1 × 10⁻⁷ M
and
[OH⁻] = 1 × 10⁻⁷ M
then:
Kᵥ = [H⁺][OH⁻]
Answer: Kᵥ = 1 × 10⁻¹⁴
Example 5 – Neutralisation

Hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide.
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
Answer: This is a neutralisation reaction.

✓ Decomposition breaks one compound into simpler substances.
✓ Thermolysis is decomposition by heat.
✓ Photolysis is decomposition by light.
✓ Electrolysis uses electricity for decomposition.
✓ Displacement involves replacement by a more reactive element.
✓ Double displacement involves exchange of ions.
✓ Precipitation produces an insoluble solid.
✓ Neutralisation produces salt and water.
✓ Reversible reactions can proceed in both directions.
✓ Equilibrium occurs when forward and reverse rates are equal.
✓ Catalysts affect reaction rate.
